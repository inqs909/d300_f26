Part A: Inner Joins

Question 1: Films and languages

Display each film’s title and the name of its language. Sort the results alphabetically by film title.

Use the film and language tables.

Question 2: Customers and addresses

Display each customer’s first name, last name, email address, street address, and postal code.

Use the customer and address tables.

Question 3: Films and categories

Display each film title and its category name. Sort the results first by category name and then by film title.

Use the film, film_category, and category tables.

Question 4: Films and actors

Display every film title along with the first and last name of each actor appearing in the film. Sort the results by film title and actor last name.

Use the film, film_actor, and actor tables.

Part B: Left Joins

Question 5: Films and inventory records

Display every film title and its inventory ID. Include films that do not have an inventory record.

Use the film and inventory tables.

Question 6: Number of inventory copies

Display every film’s ID, title, and number of inventory copies. Include films with zero inventory copies. Sort the films from the largest number of copies to the smallest.

Use the film and inventory tables.

Question 7: Films without inventory

Modify the previous query to display only films that have no inventory records.

Hint: After the left join, test a column from inventory for NULL.

Part C: Right Joins

Question 8: Preserve all films

Begin with inventory and use a RIGHT JOIN to display every film and any corresponding inventory records. Films without inventory must remain in the result.

Display the film ID, film title, inventory ID, and store ID.

Use film and inventory tables.

Part 4: Full Join

Question 9: Retain all languages and films

Use a FULL OUTER JOIN to display all languages and all films, regardless of whether a matching language-film relationship exists.

Display the language ID, language name, film ID, and film title.

Use language and film tables.

Question 10: Find unmatched languages or films

Modify the query from Question 9 to retain only unmatched records: languages without films or films without a matching language.

Part 5: Multi-table Join

Question 11: Customer rental history

Display the customer’s first and last name, film title, rental date, and return date for every rental.

Use the customer, rental, inventory, and film tables.
